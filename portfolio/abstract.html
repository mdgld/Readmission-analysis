Objectives

Predictors of psychiatric rehospitalization in children are poorly characterized, and predicting readmission in any cohort remains a lofty goal. Standard regression models failed to identify readmission predictors in our prior analysis of 2,652 children aged 5–12. In the current analysis, we used regularized and machine-learning models to identify risk factors for 90-day readmission and test their predictive performance on a small dataset.

Methods

Patient records for the biobehavioral unit were collected from 2017–2024 at one Arizona hospital. ICD-10 codes were analyzed and aggregated into DSM-5 and Clinical Classifications Software Refined (CCSR) categories. Unpenalized logistic regression provided interpretable odds ratios; regularized logistic regression, random forest (RF), and gradient boosting models were trained on clinical and demographic features with interaction terms and evaluated via bootstrap optimism correction, calibration, and fairness analysis across age, gender, insurance, and adverse childhood experience (ACE) strata.

Results

Independent associations with readmission were identified:

  • ↑ Risk Suicidal ideation — OR 1.50, p = 0.006
  • ↑ Risk Diagnostic complexity — OR 1.39 per DSM-5 category, p < 0.001
  • ↑ Risk Physical restraint use — OR 1.60, p = 0.008; also largest driver of LOS (+6 days)
  • ↑ Risk Younger age — OR 1.10 per year, p = 0.010
  • Null Modified ACE category — not associated with readmission
  • Null C-SSRS flags, NSSI, chronic medical comorbidity — not associated
  • Model RF apparent AUC 0.844 → 0.748 after bootstrap optimism correction
  • Model PPV ≈ 20% at optimal threshold — insufficient for individual triage
  • Equity Equalized-odds disparities: age gap 0.240, gender gap 0.136

Conclusions

The models surfaced risk factors that standard regression techniques failed to identify, yet individual readmission remains hard to predict — feature discovery is necessary but not sufficient for creating a useful risk measure.

These findings refine a previous report from this cohort: re-analysis shows that predictors previously reported as null emerge once a non-converging model specification was corrected. Larger, multi-site data and equity-aware validation are needed before clinical use.

Learning objectives

  1. 01 Identify the clinical factors independently associated with 90-day psychiatric readmission in children aged 5–12 — suicidal ideation, number of DSM-5-TR categories diagnosed, physical restraint use, and younger age — and distinguish them from factors not independently associated: length of stay, modified ACE risk category, C-SSRS flags, chronic medical comorbidity, and NSSI.
  2. 02 Explain how and why predictive models can surface salient risk factors yet fail to achieve clinically useful sensitivity and specificity when trained on small datasets.
  3. 03 Evaluate predictive model performance via calibration and bootstrap-corrected optimism by interpreting isotonic regression, Platt scaling, and apparent AUC relative to optimism-corrected AUC.
  4. 04 Recognize the basis of demographic-based algorithmic disparities that may arise in machine learning models, and the necessity of equity-aware validation before clinical deployment.
