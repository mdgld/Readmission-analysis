Clinical data science portfolio

Pediatric psychiatric readmission: from ICD-10 mapping to clinically honest prediction

A single-site cohort analysis of children aged 5–12 admitted for inpatient psychiatric care from 2017–2024. The project turns raw diagnosis exports into DSM-5/ICD-informed features, publication-ready figures, and an explicit assessment of what machine learning can — and cannot — predict in small clinical datasets.

Project snapshot

Why this matters

Readmission is clinically important, but individual prediction is hard.

The analysis asks a practical question: can routine clinical, demographic, and diagnosis data identify children at higher risk for psychiatric rehospitalization? The answer is nuanced. Several interpretable factors are associated with readmission, but discrimination remains modest after out-of-fold and optimism-corrected evaluation.

This portfolio page intentionally exports only aggregate statistics and generated figures. Patient-level rows, identifiers, and source CSVs are not shipped with the web build.

Workflow

  1. Cleaned raw visit-level exports into canonical patient and diagnosis tables.
  2. Mapped 1,863 ICD-10-CM codes to descriptions, ICD chapters, and DSM-5 categories.
  3. Built interpretable regression, regularized ML, bootstrap, calibration, and fairness analyses.
  4. Generated conference-facing figures and narrative summaries.

Within-category heterogeneity

Aggregate diagnostic categories conceal opposite risk signals.

A single DSM-5 label can bundle high-risk and low-risk subtypes. The interactive chart below uses a dropdown to reveal how individual ICD-10 subgroups diverge from the cohort average of 9.3%.

Subgroup risk within each DSM-5 category

Select any diagnostic category from the dropdown to see readmission rates for individual ICD-10 codes within it. Pink bars indicate elevated risk vs. cohort average; sky bars are protective; gray is non-significant.

Example: within Depressive Disorders, DMDD carries 14.2% readmission (OR 1.82) while single-episode MDD is flat at 9.0% (OR 0.96, n.s.).

Clinical interpretation: Apparent tree-model AUCs looked strong in-sample, but out-of-fold AUCs clustered near 0.64–0.68. The honest conclusion is not “deploy a readmission predictor”; it is “use modeling to surface clinically plausible risk factors, then validate in larger multi-site data.”
