Psychiatric readmission in children aged 5–12

We studied which children returned to one Arizona hospital within 90 days of discharge, and how well diagnoses, screening results and other information from their admission predicted that return.

Read the findings ↓Conference abstract

Study methods

We reviewed the records of children aged 5–12 admitted for psychiatric care from 2017 through 2024. The outcome was a return to the same hospital within 90 days of discharge. A child with multiple returns counted once.

We used regression to estimate how clinical characteristics related to readmission. In a separate analysis, we tested how well three models predicted readmission for children whose records were held out of training.

Clinical associations and model performance

We used logistic regression in the full cohort to estimate how each clinical characteristic was associated with readmission after accounting for the others. In a separate prediction analysis, all three models used the same recorded characteristics, including age, diagnoses and screening results.

How the three models work

Logistic regression
Assigns a weight to each characteristic and combines those weights to estimate the chance of readmission. In the full-cohort analysis, those weights also give us the adjusted odds ratios shown below.
Random forest
Builds many decision trees. Each tree asks a series of yes-or-no questions, such as whether a child is under 10. The trees learn from different samples of records, and the forest averages their estimates.
CatBoost
Builds a sequence of decision trees, one at a time. Each new tree tries to improve the combined predictions from the earlier trees. This approach is called boosting.

How we tested predictions

We divided the children into ten groups. Each model learned from nine groups and predicted readmission for the tenth. Each group took a turn, so every child received a prediction from a model trained on other children.

What does an AUC of 0.660 mean?

Imagine picking two children at random, one who returned and one who did not. How often did logistic regression give the higher score to the child who returned? About 66% of the time, counting ties as half. This is the area under the ROC curve (AUC). Chance alone would give 50%.
