§01 Premise

Readmission is clinically important, but individual prediction is hard

Can routine clinical, demographic, and diagnosis data identify children at higher risk for psychiatric rehospitalization? Several interpretable factors are associated with readmission — yet discrimination stays modest after out-of-fold and optimism-corrected evaluation.

The work catalogued here treats inference and prediction as complementary: machine learning is used to surface risk factors that standard regression missed, while honest out-of-fold and bootstrap-corrected metrics keep the deployment claim modest.

This website implements only aggregate statistics and generated figures. Patient-level rows, identifiers, and source CSVs are never exposed.

CLINICAL READ

Apparent tree-model AUCs looked strong in-sample — CatBoost reached 0.90 — but out-of-fold AUCs clustered near 0.64–0.68. The honest conclusion is not "deploy these readmission predictors immediately as a clinical tool”; it is "use modeling to surface clinically plausible risk factors, then validate in larger, multi-site cohorts over longer time-frames.“

§05 Equity audit

Surface subgroup disparities before deployment

Equalized-odds gaps quantify how unevenly errors fall across subgroups. Age and ACE strata show the widest gaps — equity-aware validation should be a precondition to implementation.

A model with respectable aggregate AUC can still systematically over- or under-flag a subgroup. Reporting the worst-case gap alongside the headline metric frames bias mitigation as the crucial calibration step that it is.
